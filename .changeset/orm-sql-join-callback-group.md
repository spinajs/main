Conditions appended to a join's `ON` clause can no longer escape the key match with an `OR`. Before, the conditions of a join query callback (the third argument of `leftJoin()`, `innerJoin()` and the other join methods, or `queryCallback` in the join options) were appended flat after the callback's own group, so `this.orWhere(b)` there compiled to `ON pk = fk AND ( a ) OR b` and joined every target row that matched `b` to every source row. They now compile to `ON pk = fk AND ( ( a ) OR b )`. The `whereOnJoin()` conditions of a populated relation were joined with `AND` one by one: their `OR` connectors were dropped (`where(a).orWhere(b)` compiled to `a AND b`), and a raw or lazy fragment containing `OR` escaped the key match. They now keep their connectors and are bracketed the same way: `ON pk = fk AND ( a OR b )`. A single condition that is not a raw or lazy fragment, such as a plain join callback or one `whereOnJoin()` condition, compiles as before; a lazy join callback or a single raw fragment gains harmless parentheses.
