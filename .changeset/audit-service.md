`canonicalJson` and `payloadHash` define an event's `data_hash`: the SHA-256 of the payload's canonical JSON. `audit().record()` writes an event to its trail's store in one of three ways: transactional (inside the caller's transaction, handed to the store with it), confirmed (retried per `retry`, then `onFailure` decides between `AuditWriteFailed` and a logged loss) and deferred (buffered in memory, flushed in the background, on `flush()` and on dispose; `audit.deferred`). Confirmed and deferred writes run outside the caller's transaction; `retry.attempts` counts every try, and a transactional write is never retried, always rejects with `AuditWriteFailed`, and is refused with `AuditConfigError` on a store that does not declare `joinsTransaction`. A confirmed or deferred write may name the `connection` it is written on. `audit().trail(name)` reads through the store's reader (`byActor`, `of`, `byTrace`, `since`, with `subjectType` and `order` in the query), erases with `forget`, which records its `erasure` event on the source it redacted, and has `verify()`, which a reader implements when its trail is sealed. There is one audit service per container: `audit(container)` reads that container's configuration. `LogAuditStore` writes events on the logger `audit.<trail>`; `@spinajs/audit/testing` adds `MemoryAuditStore` and `TestAuditContext`. With no `audit` configuration nothing is recorded unless a producer calls `record`.
