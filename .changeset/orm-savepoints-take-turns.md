Savepoints of one transaction take turns. A nested `transaction()` (and so a `save()` or relation `sync()` inside a transaction) waits while another savepoint of the same level is open, in call order, and a statement of the transaction waits while a savepoint is open on its level, so nothing outside a savepoint runs between `SAVEPOINT` and its release or rollback. Before, two nested calls started together (`Promise.all`) interleaved: a rollback of one undid the other's statements, and on PostgreSQL a failed statement of one could leave the transaction aborted, so the commit rolled everything back. No level ends while its queue holds work: a savepoint is released only after the nested calls it started, and `transaction()` commits or rolls back only after the savepoints and statements waiting in it ran, also when one of several parallel saves failed. A nested `transaction()` the callback started and did not await now runs inside the transaction and commits or rolls back with it, and the commit waits for it; before, its statements ran after the commit, outside the transaction, and its savepoint statements failed. `ITransactionContext` has the new optional member `level` (`ITransactionLevel`), owned by `OrmDriver`. Three new ways to hang, with no error, and on SQLite every other transaction of the process waits too: a savepoint callback that awaits work of the enclosing transaction whose statement reaches the driver only after the savepoint began (do that work before the nested `transaction()`, or inside it); a transaction callback that returns while a nested call it started waits for something that happens only after `transaction()` returned (await every nested call before the callback returns); and a statement callback (the function a driver passes to `withSessionSettings`) that opens a savepoint after its first `await` (open the savepoint before the statement starts, or after it settled).
