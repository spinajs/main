"@spinajs/orm": minor

A transaction whose statement failed with a deadlock (errno 1213), a full lock table (1206), or a lock wait timeout (1205) on a server that runs with `innodb_rollback_on_timeout`, was rolled back whole by InnoDB, and MySQL put the session back in autocommit mode. If the callback caught that error and went on, each statement after it committed by itself as it ran, `COMMIT` found nothing to commit, `transaction()` resolved and its `afterCommit()` callbacks ran: half a transaction committed and reported as a commit. Now every statement issued through such a transaction after the error (a savepoint included) is refused before it is sent with the new `TransactionAborted` (exported by `@spinajs/orm`, a subclass of `OrmException`; the server's error is its `inner`, so a retry on errno 1213 still recognises it), the commit is refused, `transaction()` rejects with it and its `afterCommit()` callbacks do not run. A statement the callback had already issued without awaiting it is sent anyway and commits on its own (after a 1205 under `innodb_rollback_on_timeout`, also one issued while the driver reads that setting, one extra round trip on every 1205): the driver cannot stop a statement already queued on the connection. A nested `transaction()` around the failing statement now rethrows the deadlock instead of the "SAVEPOINT does not exist" error of its rollback. An application that retries the failed statement inside the same transaction now gets `TransactionAborted` on every retry: retry the whole `transaction()`, and bound the loop. On a 1205 the driver reads `@@innodb_rollback_on_timeout` on that connection; with the default setting a 1205 undoes the statement only and the transaction goes on, as before.
