`audit().trail(name).verify(source?, pinned?)` now works on table and archive trails: `TableAuditReader` and `ArchiveAuditReader` recompute the seals of a sealed trail, each over its range from the end of the previous seal, and report intact and redacted events, pruned seals, the unsealed tail and the first mismatch (`payload` with the exact event, or `seal`, `count`, `chain` with the seal's range). Rows a seal does not cover are listed in `uncovered`: `late` for a row committed (or inserted with an earlier `at`) into a range after it was sealed, `pruned` for a row in a range retention removed. One late row per seal is always recognised, two or more only in small seals; otherwise, and for a row stamped after its seal was made, the seal is `count`. `unexplainedRedactions` lists blanked payloads that no `erasure` record of their subject explains; a record counts only inside a verified seal, or in the unsealed tail while it is younger than the sealing window. A `pinned` head (`{ toSeq, hash }`, from the sealer's log) must be met on the chain and protects it up to its `toSeq` only. Verification refuses to run inside an open transaction. A seal marked pruned out of chain order, or with `pruned_at` in the future, is `count`; one the trail's retention here does not explain is listed in `earlyPrunes`. Unsealed rows older than the sealing window are counted in `staleUnsealed`. An `erasure` record proves nothing against anyone who can append to the trail table. `spinajs audit:verify <trail> [--source <connection>] [--head <toSeq>:<hash>]` verifies every connection that holds the trail table (or the one named), prints one report per connection and exits with 1 when any is tampered, else 3 when one could not be verified, else 2 when one is suspect (`result: SUSPECT, ...`), else 0.
