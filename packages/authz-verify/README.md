# `@spinajs/authz-verify`

Verification of a `PolicyIR` from `@spinajs/authz-ir` (Bet 6 lane V). A development and CI tool: runtime code never imports it.

- `RewriteAutomaton`: the rewrite graph read as a finite automaton. States are `type#relation`, letters are the tuples a check walks, recursive `parent->` rewrites are cycles.
- `reachers(ir, permission)`: every holder (`role:<role>@<type>`, `<type>#<relation>`, `public:<type>#<relation>`, `anyone:<type>#<relation>` (a rewrite that holds for every principal, as an ABAC policy without targets)) that can reach the permission, with its shortest path and that path's minimal tuple set. `onlyVia` and the exceptions of `isolated` answer `unknown` for a listed anyone term the exact engine cannot decide.
- `permissionMatrix(ir)`: holder × permission → `always | conditional | never` over the holder's own paths and the paths that reach its relation through a subject set (`cells`), what every principal holds through an anyone term (`everyone`), and the opaque parts each permission depends on; `accessOf(matrix, holder, permission)` reads the two together, as `never(holder, permission)` does, and counts the row of a relation's public holder (`public:tenant#member`) for the holder that names that relation (`tenant#member`, `role:member@tenant`). A selector that is not a holder key (`tenant#member` on a role's relation, `role:member`, `public`) reads every holder it selects. A role that shares its relation with a role earlier in sort order reads as that relation's first role only, so `accessOf` can answer `never` for it where `never()` is violated.
- `implies(ir, a, b)`, `onlyVia(ir, permission, holders)`: exact verdicts on the monotone fragment (regular-language inclusion and emptiness); every `violated` verdict carries a counterexample confirmed by the reference evaluator, and non-monotone permissions answer `unknown`.
- `bounded(ir, query, { k })`: the bounded engine over `k` objects per type, through the optional peer `z3-solver` ^5 (install it in development and CI only; without it bounded verdicts report `AZV090`). Every model is re-run through the reference evaluator before it counts as a counterexample. A principal type is every direct subject type a relation names, whether the IR declares it or not (a compiled RBAC policy names `user` in `allowed` lists only); with nothing to search (no principal type, or a permission bound to a type the IR does not declare) the verdict is `unknown`, never `holds`.
- Caveats in the bounded engine: numeric, string, boolean and timestamp comparisons over the request context; pattern operators, IP addresses and condition functions are uninterpreted and reported as opaque.
- A rewrite that names a relation or a permission the IR does not define (`validateIR`'s `IR001`, and `IR005` on a declared type), or a subject set of a type the IR does not declare (`IR002`), is a part the analysis cannot see: it is listed under `opaque` as `undefined <type>#<name>`, and an assertion over a permission that reaches it is `unknown`, never proved, a `never` with no holder on any path included. `unknown` is an `AZV003` warning, so a security gate runs `authz:verify --fail-on warning`: under the default `--fail-on error` it exits 0.
- `minimize(ir, scenario, expected)`: delta debugging against the evaluator; `renderScenario(ir, name, counterexample)`: the counterexample as an `.authz.yaml` scenario.
- `defineAssertions(({ implies, separated, isolated, onlyVia, never }) => [...])` and `verifyAssertions(ir, set)`: an app's invariants, exact engine first and bounded engine where it answers `unknown`, reported as `AZV001` (violated), `AZV002` (holds), `AZV003` (opaque or undecided), `AZV090` (no solver); `formatResults(results, 'text' | 'json' | 'github')`.
- Narrowed principals: a ceiling only removes allows (its `roles` never lift a deny), and so does `delegation: 'intersect'`, so `never`, `onlyVia`, `separated`, `isolated` and the matrix hold for them too. Under `delegation: 'inherit'` the evaluator answers for the delegate, so a verdict speaks about the delegate, not about the agent's own grants. `implies(a, b)` holds for principals without a ceiling and says so with `AZV004` (info) on a policy that compiles ceilings; `implies(a, b, { narrowed: true })` checks ceilings and answers with a counterexample. A permission that only an ABAC policy permits has no path in the exact engine yet, so `implies` with it as `a`, narrowed or not, still answers `holds`.
- `outcome(ir, scenario, permissions)`: re-runs a scenario through the reference `IREvaluator`.
- `isolated(type)` is decided by the bounded engine only (`verified up to k=N`, `unknown` below `k = 2`) and checks every permission of the policy, those of `type` itself included (`tenant:enter` for `isolated('tenant')`; before this release they were reported as holding without a search). It speaks about principals whose grants all sit under one object of `type`: a global role, a grant on an object above `type` or on a group under no object of `type` is outside it. A principal that is itself an object of `type` does not cross by reaching itself. A public grant (`user:*`) reaches every principal, so it crosses wherever it sits: a policy that means public access writes `isolated('tenant', { except: ['public'] })`; a public resource under no object of `type` is outside the assertion.
- Holder selectors in the exceptions of `isolated` and the holders of `onlyVia`: one that names a relation or a role (`tenant#member`, `role:member`) selects the named holders of that relation only, not its public tuples. `isolated('tenant', { except: ['tenant#member'] })` still finds a tenant everyone may enter. Public grants on the named relation, and those behind a subject set on it at any depth (`tenant#member@group:g#member` with `group#member@user:*`), are accepted only by `'public'` or `public:tenant#member`; the bounded engine also accepts the inner spelling `public:group#member`, the exact engine reads only the outer one. A subject set that can hold no wildcard stays excused by `tenant#member`. The exact engine reads a relation as its first role's: `onlyVia(P, ['role:reader'])` with `reader` beside `auditor` on `doc#viewer` answers `violated`; list `role:auditor` or `doc#viewer` instead.
- `never(holder, permission)` with a holder that names a relation or a role (`tenant#member`, `role:member`) counts a public member of that relation as one of its holders: a public member is a member. `never({ holder: 'role:guest', permission: '*:delete' })` is violated when `doc#guest` allows `user:*` and reaches `doc:delete`, even if it allows nobody by name; the counterexample names the wildcard tuple. A public grant on another relation is not this holder's. A member behind a subject set (`tenant#member@group:g#member`), at any depth and in cycles of sets (tested on cycles of two, three and four types, with every relation of the cycle as the holder), counts in both engines for the outer relation (`tenant#member`, `role:member`, and `public:tenant#member` when the member is public) and for every relation of the sets it passes (`group#member`, `public:group#member`). The bounded search (for a permission that is not monotone) may also report a member of a relation behind the holder (`group#member` behind `tenant#member`, every subjectSet role in an RBAC policy) that reaches the permission by another way: through a tuple-to-userset, a permission of the inner relation's own type, or another role. Such a `violated` errs on the safe side; you recognise it because its counterexample has no tuple on the holder's relation, or on a set that sits on it. The assertion is then not proved either way: check that counterexample by hand, and keep the assertion out of the gate (or state it as `onlyVia`) until the bounded `never` checks that the holder is needed. A role counts every relation it holds, also one it shares with a role earlier in sort order: with `role:auditor` and `role:reader` both on `doc#viewer`, `never({ holder: 'role:reader', permission: 'doc:read' })` is violated by any member of `doc#viewer`, named or public, in both engines. This holds also where the role that sorts first holds two relations of one type: with `role:auditor` on `doc#viewer` and `doc#commenter` and `role:reader` on `doc#commenter`, `never(role:reader)` and `never(doc#commenter)` count the members of `doc#commenter`. A principal in a set that two relations allow counts for the holder only where the holder alone reaches the permission: with `p = r0 ∩ r1` and both `doc#r0` and `doc#r1` allowing `team#member`, `never(doc#r0, p)` holds, as it does for a principal named on both relations.
